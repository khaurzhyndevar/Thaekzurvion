# Thaekzurvion

> An amber door opens into the breath of a dragon that has never awakened.

## The Door on the Lake

The amber door stands upright on a frozen lake. It has no frame. Snow gathers against its lower edge, though no snow lies anywhere else on the ice.

A path of black pebbles leads from the shore. Each pebble contains a small bubble, and in every bubble a different light appears to be going out. The path can be followed only while looking at the door. Those who look down find themselves back on the bank with wet hems.

The door's handle is bone. Its hinges are flowers preserved inside the amber. Above the handle, something resembling a child's handprint remains warm throughout the winter.

In summer, the lake thaws around the door but never beneath it. From the shore, people watch fish pass through its transparent panels and emerge carrying little shadows beneath their fins.

## The Keeper's Copper Bowl

The keeper lives in a hut with no back wall. Behind it stretches a forest visible only from inside. From outside, the hut appears complete.

She gives each traveler a copper bowl containing one black pebble. They are told to carry it to the door and bring back whatever sound the bowl receives. Nobody is asked to open the door. Nobody is told that it is closed.

Most return with the sound of wind. A few bring the creak of a chair or the scrape of a spoon against porcelain. One returned with a sound the keeper recognized immediately. She buried the bowl beneath the hut and did not leave the forest for three days.

Afterward, every bubble along the path held a light that was slowly becoming brighter.

## The Traveler Who Brought a Handle

A traveler arrived carrying a second bone handle. He said it had fallen from a cupboard during a storm. The cupboard had contained nothing unusual: folded sheets, an old blanket, and a wooden horse missing one ear.

The keeper placed his handle beside her bowl. The bone acquired a fine amber sheen. Inside it appeared the forest behind the hut, viewed from a height above the trees.

She asked what had been wrapped in the old blanket. He could not remember. He remembered washing it carefully, folding it along its worn edges, and putting it away because the room had become too quiet.

She gave him the bowl without a pebble. On the lake, his path appeared as a row of dark footprints rather than stones. Each print was smaller than his own.

## Beyond the Amber

At the door, the traveler held the bowl beneath the warm handprint. A drop fell into it from the clear sky. Inside the drop he saw a long chamber lined with pale roots.

At the chamber's far end slept a dragon whose wings lay beneath its chin. Its breath moved slowly through the roots. Suspended above its mouth was a wooden horse, complete except for one ear.

The traveler placed the spare handle against the amber. The flowers inside the hinges opened. A narrow space appeared along the door's edge, releasing the smell of washed linen dried in sunlight.

Beyond that space stood no chamber. There was a small room with a cupboard, an unmade bed, and a blanket folded on a chair. On the blanket lay a little black pebble, its bubble empty of light.

## Four Marks in Warm Bone

Letters formed beneath the traveler's thumb:

*Thae — kzur — vion — ometh.*

He read them into the copper bowl. The drop of water became still. In its chamber, the dragon drew a deeper breath, and the wooden horse turned slowly above its mouth.

The traveler remembered the missing ear. He had broken it while trying to repair a loose wheel, and promised to make another before morning. He had kept the promise in his mind long after putting away the tools.

He reached into his pocket. A small piece of carved wood lay there, smooth from years of imagined handling. He placed it through the door's narrow opening.

The amber became cloudy. From the bowl came the sound of wheels crossing a wooden floor, then a laugh so brief he could not be certain it had reached the air.

## The Lake After the Crossing

The door cleared. The little room was gone. Through its panels he saw only the far shore, bright beneath a winter sun. Both bone handles had become one, and the warm handprint had faded into the amber.

He returned with the bowl held carefully against his chest. The keeper listened to it without raising it to her ear. Then she placed a black pebble inside and set it on the hut's windowsill.

He left without asking for it back. Along the shore, the snow had begun to melt in small circles around stones that had never been part of the path.

The amber door remains on the lake. Its flowers are closed again. Beyond the keeper's missing wall, a wooden wheel sometimes turns among the roots.

And deep beneath the ice, a sleeper holds one breath longer than the next, as if making room for a sound it has finally learned to keep.
